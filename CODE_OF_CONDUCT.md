# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Axium is a small project, but it's built in the open, and everyone who participates — through issues, pull requests, discussions, or any other project space — deserves to be treated with basic respect. This document sets out what that means in practice.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful of differing viewpoints, experience levels, and backgrounds.
- Give and accept constructive feedback gracefully. Disagreement about code, design, or direction is normal and welcome; it does not require personal criticism to be effective.
- Focus critique on the work, not the person behind it.
- Assume good faith. Most misunderstandings in text-based communication are exactly that — misunderstandings, not hostility.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory comments directed at any individual or group.
- Personal or political attacks unrelated to the project's actual work.
- Publishing others' private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews, or other project activity.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies to all project spaces — the repository's issues, pull requests, and discussions — as well as any other setting where someone is representing the project or its community.

## Enforcement

Violations can be reported by opening a private report through [GitHub's security advisory reporting](https://github.com/NuclearVenom/Axium/security) (used here as the available private channel, even for non-security conduct reports) or by contacting the maintainer directly through their GitHub profile, [@NuclearVenom](https://github.com/NuclearVenom).

Reports will be reviewed and handled with discretion. Depending on severity, responses may range from a private conversation to removal of contributions and a ban from further participation in the project's spaces. The maintainer will make a reasonable effort to be fair and consistent, but retains final judgment on enforcement in this repository.

## Attribution

This document was written for Axium specifically rather than adapted line-for-line from an existing template, though it follows the general shape of widely-used open-source codes of conduct such as the Contributor Covenant.
